Projects

What we are building

Two focus areas, a district data explorer, and a knowledge hub. Everything here serves forest protection and livelihoods.

Focus 1

Forest protection & climate resilience

Forests are the home, pharmacy and granary of tribal communities, and the first line of defence against a warming climate. Those who have protected them for generations are their rightful stewards.

Rising temperatures and climate change hit tribal communities, farmers, women and young people first and hardest. That is why we act now.

01

Forest & community resource rights

We support Gram Sabhas to secure Individual, Community and Community Forest Resource rights under the Forest Rights Act, 2006, patiently, lawfully and with full documentation, from the Gram Sabha to the title.

02

People-led forest protection

Conservation and regeneration run by the village: management plans, fire lines and community watch against wildfire, protection from grazing and fencing, so that every sapling has a chance to grow into a tree.

03

Water & soil security

Watershed structures, farm ponds and soil-moisture practices that hold water in the landscape for drinking, irrigation and a forest that stays green.

04

Community-led local governance

Gram Sabhas, women’s groups and local leaders plan, decide and monitor in open, inclusive and accountable forums, in the spirit of PESA and constitutional safeguards.

05

Knowledge for everyone

Aadim Desk shares Government Resolutions, Acts and guidelines in plain language, so communities, officials and NGOs work from the same facts.

Focus 2

Livelihoods that keep families rooted

Climate change reaches farmers, women and young people first. We help families earn more, risk less and stay on their land with dignity.

FromSingle-crop paddy
ToMulti-crop vegetable farming

Helping Raigad farmers diversify beyond a single paddy crop, so families have produce to sell and eat through more of the year, and are less exposed to one bad season.

Millets revival

Bringing back Nachani (finger millet) and Varai (little millet): hardy, nutritious crops suited to hill farms and a changing climate.

Organic & natural farming

Practices that lower input costs, restore soil health and strengthen nutrition and incomes for farming families.

Water for fields

Watershed structures and farm ponds that bring dependable water to agricultural land, extending the growing season.

Forest produce & local enterprise

Fair value for forest produce, and support for entrepreneurs from tribal and underserved communities, so that families need not migrate in distress to earn a living.

Skills for women & youth

Leadership and digital skills so women and young people can run enterprises, claim entitlements and lead in their Gram Sabhas.
